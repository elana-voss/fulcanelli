# Amiens

## AMIENS — The central porch as a copy of Paris

Fulcanelli notes that the central porch of Notre-Dame d'Amiens, the porch of the Saviour, reproduces almost faithfully both the motifs of the Paris portal and their order [AMIENS ¶1]. Only small details differ: at Paris the figures hold discs, here shields; the emblem of mercury is carried by a woman at Amiens and by a man at Paris [AMIENS ¶1]. He has no doubt that the hermetic work of Guillaume le Parisien influenced the decoration of the Amiens porch [AMIENS ¶1]. The building, due to Robert de Luzarches and Thomas and Renault de Cormont, was well enough preserved for restorers to respect most of its subjects [AMIENS ¶2].

## AMIENS — The wheel fire (pl. XXXIII)

The first allegory proper to Amiens is a rendering of the *feu de roue* ("wheel fire"): a seated philosopher, leaning on his right knee, seems to meditate or keep watch beside two wheels (pl. XXXIII) [AMIENS ¶3; AMIENS ¶4]. Jourdain and Duval, Ruskin, the abbé Roze and G. Durand read it as Ezekiel's vision of wheels within wheels, the prophet asleep on his knee [AMIENS ¶4]. Fulcanelli rejects this on two counts [AMIENS ¶5]: the carvers marked supernatural things with a border of clouds, which is absent here; the figure's eyes are open; and a visionary is always shown facing the apparition in surprise or ecstasy, which this man is not [AMIENS ¶6]. The wheels therefore veil something well known to the initiate [AMIENS ¶6]. The man watches, patient but a little weary: the labours of Hercules done, his work is reduced to the «ludus puerorum des textes, c’est-à-dire à l’entretien du feu» (*"children's play" of the texts, that is, the keeping of the fire*), which a woman spinning could do [AMIENS ¶6].

He reads the double wheel as «le signe de deux révolutions qui doivent agir successivement sur le composé pour lui assurer un premier degré de perfection» (*the sign of two revolutions that must act in succession on the compound to give it a first degree of perfection*); alternatively, as Pernety holds, the two natures in the *conversion*, also done by gentle, regular cooking [AMIENS ¶6]. In fact «la coction linéaire et continue exige la double rotation d’une même roue» (*linear and continuous coction requires the double rotation of one same wheel*), which stone cannot show, hence two interlocked wheels [AMIENS ¶7]. The first turn is the moist phase, *élixation*, in which the compound stays melted until a thin skin forms and thickens; the second, the dry phase or *assation*, ends when the contents of the egg appear calcined, granular or powdery, as crystals, sand or ash [AMIENS ¶7].

On this operation, «véritablement le sceau du Grand Œuvre» (*truly the seal of the Great Work*), an anonymous commentator says the philosopher cooks gently, in one vessel, «une seule vapeur qui s’épaissit peu à peu» (*a single vapour that thickens little by little*) [AMIENS ¶8; AMIENS n2]. On the heat: modern authors say it should start no higher than body temperature; Albert Poisson gives 50° rising to about 300° [AMIENS ¶8]. Philalèthe counts as a «chaleur tempérée» (*tempered heat*) one that keeps lead (327°) or tin (232°) molten, or more, as far as the vessels bear without breaking; from there one starts the heat proper to the kingdom where nature left the matter [AMIENS ¶8]. In his fifteenth rule, insisting the artist work on mineral not organic bodies [AMIENS ¶8], he has the water of «notre lac» (*our lake*) boil with the ashes of the tree of Hermes, night and day without cease, so that the celestial nature may rise and the terrestrial descend: «Car je vous assure que si nous ne faisons bouillir, nous ne pouvons jamais nommer notre ouvrage une cuisson, mais une digestion» (*for I assure you that if we do not boil, we can never call our work a cooking, but a digestion*) [AMIENS ¶9].

## AMIENS — The "apostate" as the athanor (pl. XXXIV)

A small subject on the right of the same porch is read by Durand, with the abbé Roze, as Apostasy (Jourdain and Duval: Inconstancy), a replica of the seventh Paris medallion: a tonsured cleric throws down his breeches and shoes and walks away from a little church with a cylindrical, overhanging belfry (pl. XXXIV) [AMIENS ¶10; AMIENS ¶11]. Durand adds parallels at Paris and Chartres, where the figure wears only his shirt [AMIENS ¶11].

Fulcanelli sees no link with the Paris motif: that one shows the start of the Work, this one its completion [AMIENS ¶12]. «L’église est plutôt un athanor» (*The church is rather an athanor*), and its belfry, built against all rules of architecture, is «le four secret renfermant l’oeuf philosophal» (*the secret furnace holding the philosophical egg*), with openings to watch the phases [AMIENS ¶12]. The overlooked detail is the hollow arch in the base: it is hard to accept that a church would be built on open vaults, as if on four feet [AMIENS ¶12]. Nor is the soft mass the man points at easily taken for a garment; so Fulcanelli reads the motif as hermetic, showing the coction and its apparatus: the alchemist points with his right hand at the coal sack, and his discarded shoes show «jusqu’où doivent être poussés la prudence et le souci du silence dans cette besogne cachée» (*how far prudence and care for silence must be carried in this hidden task*) [AMIENS ¶12]. The Chartres figure's light dress is explained by the furnace heat: at the fourth degree of fire, by the dry way, about 1200° must be kept, as also in projection [AMIENS ¶12]. He asks the authors why apostates should strip when leaving the temple [AMIENS ¶12]. As at Paris, the athanor takes the form of a turret on vaults; it shows the parts of the alchemical furnace, «cendrier, tour et dôme» (*ash-pit, tower and dome*), and old prints such as the woodcuts in Jean Liébaut's treatise draw furnaces as keeps and small fortresses [AMIENS ¶13; AMIENS n4].

## AMIENS — The cock and the fox (pl. XXXV)

On the pier of the great porch, a cock perches on an oak branch that a fox tries to reach (pl. XXXV), an allegory dear to Basile Valentin [AMIENS ¶14]. Profane readers see a medieval fable; Durand notes the dogs of the fable are missing, and Fulcanelli argues the exact old carvers would not have left them out had it been a fable [AMIENS ¶14].

He develops it further than at Paris: the cock is to the oak as son to father [AMIENS ¶15]. «le coq et le renard ne sont qu’un même hiéroglyphe recouvrant deux états physiques distincts d’une même matière» (*the cock and the fox are only one hieroglyph covering two distinct physical states of one same matter*) [AMIENS ¶15]. The cock is the volatile part extracted from the *subject*, whose emblem is the oak: the spring at the foot of the sacred tree, which the old philosophers called *Mercury* though it looks nothing like common quicksilver: «Car l’eau dont nous avons besoin est sèche, ne mouille pas les mains et jaillit sous le choc de la verge d’Aaron» (*for the water we need is dry, does not wet the hands, and springs at the stroke of Aaron's rod*) [AMIENS ¶15]. The mercury contains everything the work needs, as Geber's praise of it confirms; it is the only matter needed [AMIENS ¶15]. The cock, emblem of *Mercury* among the pagans and of the *resurrection* among Christians, can become the *Phénix*, but must first take the provisional fixity signified by the *goupil*, the hermetic fox [AMIENS ¶15].

Held long enough in the fire by the right *means*, this dry water becomes fixed and takes the fox as its sign: «L’eau est devenue terre et le mercure soufre» (*The water has become earth and the mercury sulphur*) [AMIENS ¶15]. But by an old axiom «toute teinture sèche est inutile en sa siccité» (*every dry tincture is useless in its dryness*), so the earth is redissolved in the water that bore it, «dans son propre sang» (*in its own blood*), and the fox takes back the cock's wings [AMIENS ¶15]. A second coagulation follows, this time resisting the fire in fusion; this gives the first stone, fairly permanent in fire, very penetrating and fusible, qualities raised by a third reiteration [AMIENS ¶15]. Then the cock, attribute of Saint Peter, the true and flowing *pierre* (stone) on which the Christian edifice rests, «le coq aura chanté trois fois» (*the cock will have crowed three times*); Peter holds «deux clefs entrecroisées de la solution et de la coagulation» (*two crossed keys of solution and coagulation*), is the symbol of the volatile stone that fire makes fixed and dense by precipitating it, and was crucified head downward [AMIENS ¶15].

## AMIENS — The citadel, the dead trees and the first agent (pl. XXXVI)

On the north (Saint-Firmin) portal, a bolted citadel with crenellated towers and a barred vent [AMIENS ¶16] may be the symbol of the esotericism of the 115 other quatrefoils, «l’idée mère de la Forteresse alchimique, reprise et modifiée par Khunrath en 1609» (*the parent idea of the Alchemical Fortress, taken up and modified by Khunrath in 1609*), or the closed *Palace* of the king of the Art of Basile Valentin and Philalèthe [AMIENS ¶17]. Below it, dead trees twist under a sky still showing sun, moon and stars (pl. XXXVI) [AMIENS ¶19]. These are the first matters, «planètes métalliques dont le feu, nous disent les Philosophes, a causé la mort, et que la fusion a rendues inertes» (*metallic planets whose death, the Philosophers tell us, was caused by fire, and which fusion has left inert*), like trees in winter [AMIENS ¶20]. Hence the Masters' advice to *réincruder* them, giving back with fluid form «l’agent propre qu’elles ont perdu dans la réduction métallurgique» (*the proper agent they lost in metallurgical reduction*) [AMIENS ¶21].

Where to find this agent is the great mystery, which Fulcanelli says he has scattered across the emblems for the perceptive seeker, never mixing in false statements as the old authors did, and using the cabala to say what his predecessors did not, keeping silence where his oath forbade more [AMIENS ¶21]. Before the *Secret des Secrets*, the *Verbum dimissum* that Saint Paul says was hidden from all ages [AMIENS ¶22; AMIENS ¶23], he cites the masters [AMIENS ¶24]. «Le Chaos métallique produit des mains de la nature contient en soy tous les métaux et n’est point métail» (*The metallic Chaos produced by nature's hands contains all metals and is not metal*) [AMIENS ¶25; AMIENS n6]. Haymon sends the seeker «à la partie postérieure du monde, là où l’on entend gronder le tonnerre, souffler le vent, tomber la grêle et la pluie» (*to the hind part of the world, where thunder rumbles, wind blows, hail and rain fall*) [AMIENS ¶26]. The commentator of the *Lumière sortant des Ténèbres* says Ægidius de Vadis shows that there remains in this world «une portion de ce premier Chaos, connue, mais méprisée d’un chacun, et qui se vend publiquement» (*a portion of that first Chaos, known but despised by everyone, and sold publicly*); the same commentator says the subject is to be sought in the mineral kingdom only [AMIENS ¶28]. Nicolas Valois's stone «est dite pierre et n’est pas pierre» (*is called stone and is not stone*) [AMIENS ¶29]; Flamel's is hidden in a fountain, «vile, abjecte et nullement prisée» (*vile, abject and not at all prized*) [AMIENS ¶30]; Le Breton names a mineral «lequel contient abondamment le fixe et le volatil» (*which contains the fixed and the volatile in abundance*) [AMIENS ¶31]. An anonymous author says the Philosophers rightly hid the mystery from those who value things only by their use, for if the Matter, which God hid in things that seem *useful* to them, were openly shown them, they would no longer value it [AMIENS ¶32].

## AMIENS — The celestial dew, the Nostoc and the night (pl. XXXVII)

On the porch of the Virgin-Mother, an Adept watches «la rosée céleste» (*the celestial dew*) fall on a mass that many take for a fleece; Fulcanelli allows it may be the mineral called *Magnésie* or philosophical *Aimant* (Magnet), since the water falls on it alone, showing a hidden «vertu attractive» (*attractive virtue*) (pl. XXXVII) [AMIENS ¶34]. The relief thus renders «la condensation de l’esprit universel» (*the condensation of the universal spirit*) [AMIENS ¶34].

He corrects the error of puffers who took the *Nostoc* literally [AMIENS ¶35]. This green, jelly-like growth appears suddenly, swells with night dew and vanishes in the sun; these traits made the Philosophers take it as «type hiéroglyphique de leur matière» (*hieroglyphic type of their matter*), and the mass in the Amiens quatrefoil is such a heap, symbol of the Sages' mineral *Magnésie* [AMIENS ¶35]. Its many names (*Archée céleste, Crachat de Lune, Beurre de terre, Graisse de rosée, Vitriol végétal, Flos coeli*) meant only their mineral principle [AMIENS ¶35]. The word comes from Greek νυξ, νυχτος, Latin *nox*, night: «C’est donc une chose qui naît la nuit, a besoin de la nuit pour se développer et ne peut se travailler que la nuit» (*It is therefore a thing born at night, which needs the night to develop and can be worked only at night*); so *notre sujet* is hidden from profane eyes, though easily recognised and worked by those who know the laws of nature exactly [AMIENS ¶36].

Since the artist seeks a generation, not a creation, the primary condition is «l’absence totale de toute lumière solaire, même diffuse ou tamisée» (*the total absence of all sunlight, even diffuse or filtered*) [AMIENS ¶37]. He argues from gestation, seeds, night dew, mushrooms, sleep and digestion, eggs hatched in light dying, and from minerals: silver salts decomposing in light, chlorine and hydrogen combining in it, ferrous sulphate turning ferric [AMIENS ¶37]. So «le soleil est le destructeur par excellence de toutes les substances trop jeunes, trop faibles pour résister à son pouvoir igné» (*the sun is the destroyer par excellence of all substances too young, too weak to resist its fiery power*), as phototherapy shows [AMIENS ¶38]. Work by day if you like, he says; Isis is mother of all things and alone dispenses Revelation and Initiation; one reaches Jesus only through his Mother, and the Virgin stands on the lunar crescent, dressed in blue, colour of the night star [AMIENS ¶39].

## AMIENS — The star (pl. XXXVIII)

Left of the same porch, a master shows three disciples the *astre hermétique*, the traditional star that guides the Philosophers and announces the birth of «fils du soleil» (*son of the sun*) (pl. XXXVIII) [AMIENS ¶40]. He recalls Nicolas Rollin's rebus device, painted in 1477 on the floor of the Beaune hospital, which showed its owner's science by the sign of the Work, «la seule étoile» (*the only star*) [AMIENS ¶40].
