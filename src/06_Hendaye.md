# The Cyclic Cross of Hendaye

## HENDAYE — The cross of the end of the cycle

A plain stone cross beside the parish church of Hendaye, a small Basque border town, was moved there from the cemetery in 1842; its origin is unknown, and Fulcanelli dates it from its base and column to no earlier than the late seventeenth or early eighteenth century [HENDAYE ¶1; HENDAYE ¶2]. It is for him the strangest monument of primitive millenarianism, the rarest symbolic rendering of chiliasm he has met, a doctrine he places among the esoteric traditions of the philosophy of Hermès [HENDAYE ¶2]. The reliefs are naive and clumsily carved, not a professional's work, yet their unknown maker, he holds, had deep science and real cosmographic knowledge [HENDAYE ¶3].

The crossbar of this Greek cross carries the common salutation to the cross as sole hope, cut on two lines so that the second begins with *pes*, "foot", instead of *spes*, "hope", which also breaks the grammar, since *pes* is masculine and its adjective feminine [HENDAYE ¶4; HENDAYE ¶5; HENDAYE ¶6; HENDAYE ¶7]. Because the other motifs are carefully placed and the letters are clean and do not overlap (pl. XLVII), Fulcanelli rejects a slip of the carver and takes the apparent error as a sign set there on purpose to rouse the observer's curiosity [HENDAYE ¶7]. He says the pedestal had already given him the key, and he argues from the inscription only to show what common sense and logic can do [HENDAYE ¶8].

The serpent-shaped S answers to the Greek *khi* (X) and takes its esoteric meaning: the helical track of the sun at the zenith of its curve at the cyclic catastrophe, and an image of the beast of the Apocalypse, the dragon that pours fire and sulphur on creation at the Judgement [HENDAYE ¶9]. It tells him to read the line in the secret language, the language of the gods or of the birds, by the rules of Diplomatics, which he says Grasset d’Orcet set out clearly: read the Latin as written but in French, permute the vowels, and from the sounds rebuild a new sentence with its spelling and word order restored [HENDAYE ¶9]. The result is a warning: «Il est écrit que la vie se réfugie en un seul espace» (*It is written that life takes refuge in a single space*) [HENDAYE ¶9]. A note takes "space" in the sense of a place, a country or territory [HENDAYE n1]. So, Fulcanelli concludes, there is a land where death will not reach man at the time of the double cataclysm, and finding where it lies is left to us [HENDAYE ¶9]. From there the elect will see the golden age return; the elect, children of Elie, will be saved as Scripture says, raised by faith and perseverance to be disciples of the Christ-Light, will bear his sign and tie regenerated mankind back to the traditions of the vanished one [HENDAYE ¶9].

The front face bears INRI and corresponds to the cycle figure on the base (pl. XLVIII), so the monument holds two crosses: above, the divine cross, the means of expiation; below, the cross on the globe, marking the pole of the northern hemisphere and the time of that expiation [HENDAYE ¶10]. God the Father holds this globe topped with the fiery sign, and the sovereigns of the four great centuries, historical figures of the four ages of the world, are shown with the same attribute: Alexandre, Auguste, Charlemagne, Louis XIV [HENDAYE ¶10]. A note adds that the last was only a king, the Sun-King, marking the decline of the star before the long cyclic night [HENDAYE n2]. Read openly, INRI names Jesus of Nazareth, King of the Jews; taken from the cross, its secret sense is that nature is renewed whole by fire, for our hemisphere will soon be tried in fire, and the good will be parted from the wicked as fire parts gold from impure metals [HENDAYE ¶10].

The four faces of the pedestal show the sun, the moon, a great star, and the initiates' figure of the solar cycle: a circle quartered by two perpendicular diameters, each quarter marked with an A for one of the four ages of the world [HENDAYE ¶11]. Fulcanelli calls it a complete hieroglyph of the universe, joining heaven and earth, macrocosm and microcosm, the cross of redemption and the circle of the world [HENDAYE ¶11]. Medieval art showed the four phases by the evangelists, the Greek alpha, or most often the four evangelical animals round Christ, the human and living figure of the cross, set apart from them by the mystic almond, as at Chartres, Le Mans, Luz, Civray and Saint Trophine d’Arles (pl. XLIX) [HENDAYE ¶12]. He cites saint Jean's four animals before the throne, like a lion, a calf, a man and an eagle, and the matching account of Ezéchiel [HENDAYE ¶13]. Hindu myth sets on the quartered circle a cow, Virtue, standing on four feet in the first age, the *Crédayougam*, and on only one in the *Calyougam*, our iron age of misery and decrepitude [HENDAYE ¶14].

The iron age bears only the seal of Death: the skeleton with Saturn's attributes, the empty hourglass of time run out and the scythe, echoed in the figure seven, number of transformation and destruction [HENDAYE ¶15]. Its Gospel is that of saint Matthieu, whose name Fulcanelli derives from the Greek for "science": the Gospel according to Science, last of all but first for us, since it teaches that all but a few elect must perish together [HENDAYE ¶15]. Hence the angel given to saint Matthieu: science alone can give man wings to rise to the highest truths and reach God [HENDAYE ¶15].
