# The Book as a Whole

## The thesis

Fulcanelli's claim is that the Gothic cathedral is a book. It is one of the «beaux livres d’images dressés sur nos parvis» (*fine picture books set up on our church squares*) [INTRO.1 ¶1; INTRO.1 ¶2], and unlike written books it conveys a single, absolute meaning, simply expressed [INTRO.1 ¶4]. It is an encyclopedia of medieval knowledge. What draws him most in it is the symbolism of the old alchemists, which the medieval sculptors rendered skilfully [INTRO.2 ¶3; INTRO.2 ¶4]. Notre-Dame de Paris is his type [INTRO.9 ¶1]. Its central trumeau puts Alchemy in the place of honour, as «le sceau du Grand Oeuvre laïque au front du Grand Oeuvre chrétien» (*the seal of the lay Great Work on the brow of the Christian Great Work*) [PARIS.1 ¶3; PARIS.1 ¶4]. The cathedral, he says, is founded on alchemy. Stripped of her veil, the Virgin-Mother to whom it is dedicated personifies the primitive substance that the creating Principle used [PARIS.1 ¶5]. The whole building is a mute but pictured glorification of the science of Hermes [PARIS.1 ¶12]. Time, vandals and restorers have destroyed much, but enough survives to find the hidden arcanum again [INTRO.2 ¶7; INTRO.2 ¶8]. He holds that this science is as exact as optics or geometry [PARIS.7 ¶29]. The book thus does two things: it shows how to read the monuments, and it uses that reading to set out the Great Work; Paris closes on the paradox of «le flambeau de l’alchimique pensée illuminant le temple de la pensée chrétienne» (*the torch of alchemical thought lighting the temple of Christian thought*) [PARIS.5 ¶19; PARIS.5 ¶20].

## How the parts build it

**Canseliet's prefaces** (1925, 1957, 1964) [PREF1 ¶13; PREF2 ¶46; PREF3 ¶51] frame the book but are not part of its argument. The first says that, on the Author's assurance, one of the book's figures gives the key of the major arcanum, and that this key is a colour shown to the worker from the first labour [PREF1 ¶11]. The second explains the Master's anonymity [PREF2 ¶2]. It also develops the double star [PREF2 ¶21; PREF2 ¶26] and credits the book with restoring the phonetic *cabale* [PREF2 ¶45]. The third defends the fertile putrefaction [PREF3 ¶15] and Fulcanelli's correction of Cambriel over the statue of Saint Marcel [PREF3 ¶48].

**The Introduction** sets up the frame of reading. The church plan is a cross, the hieroglyph of the crucible [INTRO.4 ¶1]. The labyrinth stands for the whole labour of the Work [INTRO.5 ¶3]. The three roses display the colours, and the rose window stands for the fire and its duration [INTRO.6 ¶2; INTRO.6 ¶12]. The Star of the Magi shines on the compost [INTRO.6 ¶13]. The Black Virgins of the crypts stand for the primitive earth [INTRO.8 ¶4], and Saint Christopher is the one "who carries gold" [INTRO.9 ¶15].

**Paris** carries the main weight. At the base of the façade one follows the work of the Elixir from first calcination to final coction [PARIS.2 ¶1]. The sequence runs as follows:
- the Fountain, or solvent, and the athanor [PARIS.2 ¶6; PARIS.2 ¶25];
- twelve figures giving the black, Mercury, the secret fire, the colours, the conjunction and the Lions [PARIS.3; PARIS.4];
- medallions on the Solution, which alone shows which of two ways is followed [PARIS.5 ¶14];
- the north portal, on the universal Spirit made bodily, the May Dew [PARIS.6 ¶23; PARIS.6 ¶24];
- the south portal, on the short dry way [PARIS.7 ¶1];
- Flamel's buildings and the Saint-Thomas escutcheon [PARIS.8 ¶7].

**Amiens** repeats the Paris porch almost exactly [AMIENS ¶1] and adds what Paris lacks:
- the coction as the "wheel fire" [AMIENS ¶6; AMIENS ¶7];
- the athanor at the end of the Work [AMIENS ¶12];
- the cock and fox as one matter in two states [AMIENS ¶15];
- the dead metals that must be revived (*réincruder*) [AMIENS ¶20; AMIENS ¶21];
- the night as the condition of generation [AMIENS ¶37];
- the star [AMIENS ¶40].

**Bourges** moves from church to house. Jacques Cœur's palace yields the stone at the white and Mercury as pilgrim [BOURGES.1 ¶3; BOURGES.1 ¶5]. The Hôtel Lallemant yields:
- the vessel [BOURGES.2 ¶4];
- the Sign [BOURGES.2 ¶14];
- the Epiphany cake [BOURGES.2 ¶14];
- the Golden Fleece [BOURGES.2 ¶20];
- the riddle RERE, RER [BOURGES.2 ¶33; BOURGES.2 ¶34].

Its author entrusted his science to stone rather than vellum [BOURGES.2 ¶37].

**The later Hendaye chapter** reads a cross no older than the late seventeenth century [HENDAYE ¶2]. It turns the same method from the Work to the cycle of the world: nature renewed whole by fire, and life taking refuge in a single place [HENDAYE ¶9; HENDAYE ¶10].

**The Conclusion** states what the worker must be [CONCL ¶7].

## The threads

### The key of reading

Fulcanelli reads by sound. «art gothique n’est qu’une déformation orthographique du mot argotique, dont l’homophonie est parfaite» (*Gothic art is only a misspelling of the word argotique, whose homophony is perfect*) [INTRO.3 ¶5]. Argot is a spoken cabala, and it derives from the Language of the Birds [INTRO.3 ¶5; INTRO.3 ¶8]. The task is to dissect words, break their bark and free their spirit [INTRO.3 ¶7]. The same rule gives several readings:
- Christophe as Chrysophe [INTRO.9 ¶15];
- Kermès as Hermès [BOURGES.2 ¶22];
- RERE as REBIS [BOURGES.2 ¶33];
- the Hendaye *pes* for *spes* [HENDAYE ¶7; HENDAYE ¶9].

Beside the cabala stands a rule of exact looking. At the Paris porch he declines two biblical subjects, to avoid any charge of arbitrary interpretation, and keeps to motifs whose profane character is beyond doubt [PARIS.2 ¶26]. He sets aside the Ezekiel reading at Amiens because of what the carving shows: no clouds and open eyes [AMIENS ¶6]. He holds that Saint Marcel's gesture of silence "is not and never was" [PARIS.7 ¶35].

### The matter

The first matter is a black, heavy, brittle ore from the mines, hence the black underground Virgins [INTRO.8 ¶4]. It is the rough cornerstone, often given the devil's image [INTRO.4 ¶5; INTRO.4 ¶6]. The authors he quotes call it the *Miroir de l’Art* [PARIS.5 ¶4]. A commentator he cites says a portion of the first Chaos remains, despised by everyone and sold publicly, and that the subject is to be sought in the mineral kingdom only [AMIENS ¶28]. The oak names its common name [BOURGES.2 ¶21], but the name itself is never written [BOURGES.2 ¶23].

### The solvent and the secret fire

The hidden Fountain is the solvent that penetrates all metals [PARIS.2 ¶6]. Its agent is a secret fire that looks more like a water than a flame. Fulcanelli calls it the highest secret of the Work and says it stopped him for more than twenty years [PARIS.3 ¶20]. It is the universal Spirit, which must be caught in an attractive body, the magnet in the belly of Aries [PARIS.4 ¶13; PARIS.4 ¶16], and condensed as May Dew [PARIS.6 ¶24]. The first magnetic agent used to prepare the solvent is the Green Lion [PARIS.4 ¶17]. With it, gold and the dead metals can be brought back to their first state [BOURGES.1 ¶10; AMIENS ¶21]. Theseus, Tristan, Saint George and Saint Marcel are all figures of the single combat that yields this solvent [BOURGES.1 ¶9].

### The stages and signs

The essential colours follow a fixed order: black, white, red [PARIS.3 ¶25]. «Le Corbeau est, en quelque sorte, le sceau canonique de l'Œuvre, comme l’étoile est la signature du sujet initial» (*The Raven is, as it were, the canonical seal of the Work, as the star is the signature of the initial subject*) [PARIS.3 ¶3]. Yet Fulcanelli warns: «Ne vous en rapportez point trop à la couleur» (*Do not trust too much to the colour*) [PARIS.3 ¶33]. A regime is a way of feeding the stone's life, which need not show as a sequence of colours [PARIS.3 ¶35]. Its main operations (the order of this list is ours, not the book's):
1. **Sublimations.** The first conjunction is made by repeated sublimations, the "eagles"; Philalèthe asks for no fewer than seven and up to nine [PARIS.4 ¶3].
2. **The Sign.** The prepared mercury must bear the Sign, a criss-cross on its surface, which the old authors also called the Star of the Magi [BOURGES.2 ¶14]; compare the star on the compost [INTRO.6 ¶13].
3. **Coction.** The coction is a wheel fire turning twice, first moist, then dry [AMIENS ¶7].
4. **Three repetitions.** One technique, repeated three times, gives the Sulphur, the Elixir and the Stone [BOURGES.2 ¶32].
5. **Multiplications.** Later cookings with the Sulphur, the multiplications, let the mercury transmute in quantity [PARIS.7 ¶27].

Through it all, as the Philosophers say, «le Mercure seul suffit» (*Mercury alone suffices*) [PARIS.3 ¶18; BOURGES.2 ¶28]. There are two ways. In the first, alchemical gold is dissolved by the Alkaest; in the second, common gold is dissolved by *notre mercure* [PARIS.5 ¶14]. The south portal sets a short dry way, worked in a crucible, against a longer wet way [PARIS.7 ¶3; PARIS.7 ¶4; PARIS.7 ¶7]. Generation needs total darkness [AMIENS ¶37]. Nothing succeeds without knowledge of the vessel [BOURGES.2 ¶34].

### The worker

The ladder of nine rungs on the trumeau stands for patience [PARIS.1 ¶3]. Once the labours of Hercules are done, the work is "children's play", the keeping of the fire [AMIENS ¶6]. It also calls for prudence and silence [AMIENS ¶12]. The artist must make his own vessel [BOURGES.2 ¶34]. He is "Pilgrim first, pilot afterwards" [BOURGES.1 ¶5]. The Conclusion asks for:
- exact observation and a sound mind;
- a pure heart;
- indifference to received theories;
- seeking truth from Nature [CONCL ¶7].

Knowing comes from observation, power from imitation of Nature, and daring from patience, will and faith [CONCL ¶8–¶13]. The Adept who succeeds serves the humble and the suffering, and keeps silent [CONCL ¶14; CONCL ¶15; CONCL ¶16]. Fulcanelli praises Lallemant's modesty and sincerity [BOURGES.2 ¶38]. He explains what he does because «un livre qui n’apprend rien est inutile et vain» (*a book that teaches nothing is useless and vain*) [BOURGES.2 ¶13].

## What the book leaves unsaid

Fulcanelli says openly how he handles the great mystery. He has scattered the agent across the emblems for the perceptive seeker, never mixed in false statements, and kept silent where his oath forbade more [AMIENS ¶21]. Each open point is named as such:
- He stops at the body that yields the May Dew, calling it the *Verbum dimissum* [PARIS.6 ¶25].
- He will not reveal the secret fire [PARIS.3 ¶20].
- He gives the subject's common name only by hint (the cake, the cat), never in words [BOURGES.2 ¶23], and cannot speak more plainly of the oak [BOURGES.2 ¶21].
- He may not fully unveil RER, the vessel [BOURGES.2 ¶34].
- He leaves the Paris Lion undecided, since its paint is gone [PARIS.4 ¶19].
- He leaves to the reader where the land of refuge lies [HENDAYE ¶9].

Canseliet adds two silences of his own. He does not say which figure gives the key of the colour [PREF1 ¶11]. And he holds that the Master's real name stays hidden behind the pseudonym [PREF2 ¶2]. The book ends under the motto «Scire. Potere. Audere. Tacere.» (*To know. To be able. To dare. To be silent.*) and the sentence «La Nature n’ouvre pas à tous, indistinctement, la porte du sanctuaire.» (*Nature does not open the door of the sanctuary to all without distinction.*) [CONCL ¶1; CONCL ¶2; CONCL ¶3].
