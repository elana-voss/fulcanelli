# Introduction

Fulcanelli's *Le Mystère des Cathédrales* (1926) reads the Gothic cathedrals and other monuments as books in stone that teach the Great Work of alchemy. This site condenses the book in English, part by part, and states in plain terms what it teaches.

**The French text is the only source.** Everything here was written from the French text of the third edition (1964), with no outside knowledge about Fulcanelli, the monuments or alchemy, and without using any published translation. All English renderings are ours.

**References.** Every statement points to the French text: `[PARIS.3 ¶12]` is section 3 of the Paris part, paragraph 12; `[PARIS.3 n2]` is its footnote 2. Quotes are given in French, verbatim, followed by our English.

**What is here**

- One page per part of the book: Canseliet's three prefaces, the introductory part, Paris, Amiens, Bourges, the Cyclic Cross of Hendaye, and the Conclusion.
- *The Book as a Whole*: the argument of the book in one page.
- Two method pages: **A**, how Fulcanelli reads the monuments; **B**, the Great Work that reading describes.
- A lexicon of the book's terms, persons, works and places: English headword, the French form as the book spells it, and how the English was arrived at.
- A list of the plates with their captions and the passages that cite them.

**Rights.** Fulcanelli's 1926 text is in the public domain and is quoted as needed. Canseliet's prefaces and the Hendaye chapter, published later, are still protected: they are summarised in our own words with at most one short quote per point. The plates are not reproduced; the plate list links to where they can be seen.
