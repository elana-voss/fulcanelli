# Binding rules (they bind every stage)

## The one rule: this text is the only source
- The source is the French text of Fulcanelli's *Le Mystère des Cathédrales* (3rd edition, with Eugène Canseliet's three prefaces), given to you with paragraph ids. Use **only** what it says. Do not use anything you know about this book, Fulcanelli or his identity, Canseliet, the monuments, the authors and works cited, alchemy, symbolism, heraldry, or later commentary. No published translation of the book is used anywhere in this project.
- If you recognize a name, work, monument or term, record **only what this text says** about it. Do not add dates, identities, locations, titles, glosses or explanations the text doesn't give. Write names exactly as the text spells them.
- **Never make a decoding the text does not make.** Fulcanelli reads words by sound and by root (phonetic cabala, *langue des oiseaux*, argot, Greek and Latin etymology). Record each such reading exactly as the text gives it. Do not extend it, complete it, or invent a parallel one, however tempting.
- Test every statement you write: *can a reader verify it from the reference I give?* If not, cut it.
- Every item carries references: `PARIS.3 ¶12` (paragraph), `PARIS.3 n2` (footnote). Use the reference of the paragraph or footnote the quoted words actually stand in.

## Quotes
- Quotes are **verbatim French** (or the Latin, Greek, old French the text has): exact spelling, accents, archaic forms, punctuation, apparent typos. One contiguous span, no ellipses of your own, at most ~40 words. Copy from the chunk, never from memory. You may leave out the italics marks `*`, footnote markers `[^3]` and page markers `(48)`; everything else is copied as it stands.
- Every quote has an English rendering beside it: ours, faithful and plain, made from the French. Keep the text's capitalised technical words recognisable (e.g. *Œuvre* → "Work", *Mercure* → "Mercury") and keep a French word in italics where English has no fit (e.g. *argot*).

## Voices
Keep voices apart; where a format has a voice field, use these tags:
- `FULCANELLI` — the author's own statement or position (including a cited view he clearly adopts).
- `QUOTED: <name as given>` — a cited author, inscription, tradition or a view he reports. Note whether he endorses, qualifies or rejects it if the text shows this.
- `CANSELIET` — the voice of the prefaces (signed by Canseliet). In a preface, words he attributes to his master are `QUOTED: Fulcanelli`.
- `UNCLEAR` — when you cannot tell from the text.
Determine voice from the text itself (quotation marks, « », "dit", "écrit", the section), not from outside knowledge.

## Plain meaning
The project states, for a modern reader, (A) how Fulcanelli reads the monuments and (B) what account of the Great Work that reading yields. For every veiled expression bearing on either, give its plain meaning only as far as the text supports it:
- *Stated*: the text itself says what the expression means ("c'est-à-dire", "nous voulons dire", "signifie", "désigne", "est l'image de", "les Philosophes appellent…").
- *Interpreted*: you infer the meaning; then say so and cite the specific passages that support it. Prefer the narrowest reading the text supports.
- If several readings are defensible, give each with its basis rather than choosing.

## Translations
All English renderings are ours. Where the book itself translates a Latin, Greek or old-French passage into French, render from the book's French and say that the gloss is the book's. Translations are aids, not evidence: never build a claim on our rendering alone without saying so.
